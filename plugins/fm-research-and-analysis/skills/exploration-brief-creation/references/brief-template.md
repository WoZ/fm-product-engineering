# Exploration Brief Template

Read when assessing completeness and preparing the brief. [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns readiness, confirmation, and persistence. This template must not turn optional details into required user answers.

## Document Structure

Use one descriptive H1 and the applicable English section headings below in order. Fill the brief with current, agreed framing rather than dialogue history. Remove bracketed guidance. Omit optional sections or subsections without material content; do not invent facts or use empty placeholders.

```markdown
# [Research subject and intended understanding or decision]

## Purpose and intended use

[Why the investigation is needed, who will use it, and what understanding or decision it should enable. State the required outcome: explanation, map of options, comparison, recommendation, or a task-appropriate combination.]

## Preparation mode and context

[Existing project context, with agreed inspection limits, or Greenfield Zone. Identify the supplied clarified input. Incorporate material context so the brief stands alone.]

[Relevant decisions, available evidence, and references to inspected materials when applicable. Attribute unverified user assertions; describe any limitations of inspection.]

## Research questions and decomposition

[Main research question. Significant subquestions, why they matter, their priorities, and dependencies. Give questions stable references when useful for connecting them to the plan.]

## Scope and exclusions

[Included subjects, objects, scenarios, conditions, and material boundaries. Explicit exclusions. If discovery must identify relevant directions, state the agreed boundary and how directions will be selected.]

## Constraints and evaluation criteria

### Mandatory constraints

[Binding conditions with their qualifications.]

### Preferences

[Nonmandatory preferences and agreed priorities.]

### Evaluation criteria

[When evaluation or comparison is required: criteria, their interpretation, and agreed tradeoffs. Do not invent weights or thresholds.]

## Research plan

[Stages and dependencies; each significant question has an investigation path. For each stage or coherent group, describe the question and purpose, method, necessary data or sources and access, expected intermediate output, and sufficiency or transition condition.]

[Research resources and limits when material. State access or feasibility checks and bounded evidence-dependent branches for unknowns. Distinguish these from constraints on the eventual solution.]

[State that the researcher may refine methods and subquestions within the agreed goal, scope, constraints, and resources, documenting consequential adjustments. Changes outside these boundaries require user agreement.]

## Evidence and uncertainty

[Task-appropriate requirements for supporting claims, tracing evidence, distinguishing interpretation, addressing conflicting evidence, and reporting insufficient data.]

## Expected output and completion criteria

[Required contents and useful presentation of the research result. State which questions must be addressed, the required level of support, and conditions for ending the investigation. Explain how unresolved questions and limitations should be reported.]

## Open questions and assumptions

[Material research unknowns, unverified planning assumptions, or explicit deferrals, with status, consequence, and handling. Omit this section when none remain.]
```

## Filling and Completion Checks

- Purpose, preparation mode and context, questions, scope, plan, evidence expectations, and output/completion criteria must be present. Constraint subsections appear only when applicable.
- Preserve current input meaning and accepted clarifications without reproducing source utterances, question IDs from the dialogue, discarded proposals, or an execution log. Research question references are allowed.
- Keep the full formulation and material context in the brief; a link to the input or project materials does not substitute for them. Include useful source references without relying on paths outside the recipient's environment; explain unavailable references.
- In Greenfield Zone, do not add claims based on independent project inspection. Record context from the supplied input with its original status. The preparation mode describes brief creation; the plan must separately state whether any later project inspection is intended and agreed. Selecting Greenfield Zone does not authorize later project access.
- Use a table for plan connections only when helpful. The method, sources, output, and sufficiency criterion for a significant question must be recoverable without guessing, but need not be repeated in every section.
- Do not include research findings or present candidate approaches as established merely to make the plan look complete. Already available evidence can inform framing with its provenance and limitations.
- Allow an initial mapping or feasibility stage when evidence is needed to refine the investigation. Specify its boundaries and transition criteria rather than disguising an undefined task as a complete plan.
- Separate success criteria for a solution from completion criteria for research. A supported conclusion that no suitable approach was found can satisfy the research task. Insufficient evidence must remain visible; it is not proof that no solution exists.
- For an explicitly requested unfinished draft, insert `> Status: DRAFT — NOT READY FOR RESEARCH.` immediately after the H1 and identify blockers and missing decisions. Do not describe such a draft as confirmed and ready. Saving a draft does not complete the workflow.
- Before confirmation, check that another researcher can start from this document alone, with clear prerequisite checks and no unsupported decisions about intent or permission.
