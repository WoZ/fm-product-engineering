# Exploration Brief Template

Read when assessing completeness and preparing the brief. [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns readiness, confirmation, and persistence. This template must not turn optional details into required user answers.

## Document Structure

Use one descriptive H1 and the applicable English section headings below in order. Fill the brief with current, agreed framing rather than dialogue history. Remove bracketed guidance. Omit optional sections or subsections without material content; do not invent facts or use empty placeholders.

Apply the shared [communication and writing style](../SKILL.md#communication-and-writing-style) to the entire document. Keep the fixed headings below and preserve the task's precision.

```markdown
# [Research subject and intended understanding or decision]

## Purpose and intended use

[Why the investigation is needed, who will use it, and what understanding or decision it should enable. Where applicable, identify the primary problem without substituting its occurrence conditions or consequences. State the required outcome: explanation, map of options, comparison, recommendation, or a task-appropriate combination.]

## Preparation mode and context

[Existing project context, with agreed inspection limits, or Greenfield Zone. Identify the supplied clarified input. Incorporate material context so the brief stands alone.]

[Relevant decisions, available evidence, and references to inspected materials when applicable. Where material, distinguish the conditions under which the problem arises and its consequences, preserving supplied relationships. Attribute unverified user assertions and causal hypotheses; describe any limitations of inspection.]

## Research questions and decomposition

[Main research question, preserving the agreed task and any causal structure.]

### R1 — [Direction title]

[Research question.]

[One or two explanatory paragraphs: what will be investigated, its boundaries, plausible investigation paths or evidence-dependent branches, and which user conclusion, understanding, or decision could change depending on the answer. Explain any necessary dependency on another direction. Preserve the substance agreed during direction review.]

#### Research steps

[Numbered, substantive points describing the planned checks and analysis in order. Follow the main workflow's grouping and granularity review rules, without a fixed count. Adapt them to the actual question, scope, and methods. Preserve the current agreed steps, including user-requested combinations and removals; do not repopulate a shorter list. Describe planned work rather than findings.]

#### Expected research output

[One or two paragraphs stating the concrete information this direction should yield. Use a bullet list where enumeration improves clarity. Describe the expected output, not the purpose, research steps, or final result of the entire brief. Do not claim the research has already established an answer.]

#### Example

[A short agreed illustration of what this direction investigates. Label it hypothetical unless supported by attributed evidence; do not imply a research finding.]

[Repeat for each agreed direction in dependency order, with its stable research label only in the first line beside the title. Refer to directions by title elsewhere. Include agreed priorities and dependencies without duplicating the plan.]

## Scope and exclusions

[Included subjects, objects, scenarios, conditions, and material boundaries. Explicit exclusions. If discovery must identify relevant directions, state the agreed boundary and how directions will be selected.]

## Constraints and evaluation criteria

### Mandatory constraints

[Binding conditions with their qualifications.]

### Preferences

[Nonmandatory preferences and agreed priorities.]

### Evaluation criteria

[When evaluation or comparison is required: criteria, their interpretation, and agreed tradeoffs. Do not invent weights or thresholds.]

## Research plan

[Stages and dependencies; each significant question has an investigation path. For each stage or coherent group, describe the question and purpose, method, necessary data or sources and access, expected intermediate output, and sufficiency or transition condition.]

[Research resources and limits when material. State access or feasibility checks and bounded evidence-dependent branches for unknowns. Distinguish these from constraints on the eventual solution.]

[State that the researcher may refine methods and subquestions within the agreed goal, scope, constraints, and resources, documenting consequential adjustments. Changes outside these boundaries require user agreement.]

## Evidence and uncertainty

[Task-appropriate requirements for supporting claims, tracing evidence, distinguishing interpretation, addressing conflicting evidence, and reporting insufficient data.]

## Expected output and completion criteria

[Required contents and useful presentation of the research result. State which questions must be addressed, the required level of support, and conditions for ending the investigation. Explain how unresolved questions and limitations should be reported.]

## Open questions and assumptions

[Material research unknowns, unverified planning assumptions, or explicit deferrals, with status, consequence, and handling. Omit this section when none remain.]
```

## Filling and Completion Checks

- Purpose, preparation mode and context, questions, scope, plan, evidence expectations, and output/completion criteria must be present. Constraint subsections appear only when applicable.
- Preserve current input meaning and accepted clarifications without reproducing source utterances, question IDs from the dialogue, discarded proposals, or an execution log. Refer to research directions by title outside their first heading line.
- Preserve the distinction between the primary problem, its occurrence conditions, and its consequences wherever applicable. Keep unverified causal explanations labeled as hypotheses; do not invent a problem or causal chain for a task that has none.
- Retain each individually confirmed direction with one or two explanatory paragraphs, a `Research steps` section reflecting the current agreed list without a count requirement, a separate `Expected research output` section of one or two paragraphs or a useful bullet list, and a short example. A bare list of questions is insufficient. Preserve the agreed substance; substantive changes require renewed individual review of affected directions.
- Check that local research steps follow the main workflow's grouping and granularity rules, serve the expected output, and fit the overall plan. Preserve agreed reductions; resolve any resulting substantive gaps through clarification rather than silently restoring removed work. Keep shared methods, dependencies, resources, and transitions in the overall plan rather than repeating all local lists there.
- Before presenting the brief for confirmation, review every section against the shared communication and writing style. Preserve technical precision and the status of conditions, preferences, and hypotheses.
- Check directions together for duplicated work and conflicting scope, assumptions, or outputs; resolve material conflicts before confirmation. A direction being revised may be shown in full as a working version with inline issue notes and clarification questions, but cannot be confirmed with blockers. Follow the main workflow when a change reopens earlier answers. Treat deliberate research alternatives as alternatives, not inconsistencies. Place directions whose definitions or user clarifications affect others first, and reflect the agreed dependencies in the plan.
- Check the meaningful effect of every agent-added direction on the user's conclusion or decision, directly or through a stated dependency. Remove unjustified additions or keep only a short contextual explanation without separate research work. Changes to user-requested directions require user agreement.
- Keep the full formulation and material context in the brief; a link to the input or project materials does not substitute for them. Include useful source references without relying on paths outside the recipient's environment; explain unavailable references.
- In Greenfield Zone, do not add claims based on independent project inspection. Record context from the supplied input with its original status. The preparation mode describes brief creation; the plan must separately state whether any later project inspection is intended and agreed. Selecting Greenfield Zone does not authorize later project access.
- Use a table for plan connections only when helpful. The method, sources, output, and sufficiency criterion for a significant question must be recoverable without guessing, but need not be repeated in every section.
- Do not include research findings or present candidate approaches as established merely to make the plan look complete. Already available evidence can inform framing with its provenance and limitations.
- Allow an initial mapping or feasibility stage when evidence is needed to refine the investigation. Specify its boundaries and transition criteria rather than disguising an undefined task as a complete plan.
- Separate success criteria for a solution from completion criteria for research. A supported conclusion that no suitable approach was found can satisfy the research task. Insufficient evidence must remain visible; it is not proof that no solution exists.
- For an explicitly requested unfinished draft, insert `> Status: DRAFT — NOT READY FOR RESEARCH.` immediately after the H1 and identify blockers and missing decisions. Do not describe such a draft as confirmed and ready. Saving a draft does not complete the workflow.
- Before confirmation, check that another researcher can start from this document alone, with clear prerequisite checks and no unsupported decisions about intent or permission. Incorporate newly agreed answers into the current brief without automatically rewriting the source input or retaining obsolete conflicting conditions. Working-version notes must be resolved before the direction or complete brief is presented as ready; intentionally saved drafts retain visible blockers under the draft rule above.
